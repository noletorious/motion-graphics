# Exposure plan

How Motion turns into an audience: blog (home base), Substack (email), LinkedIn (reach), short video (discovery). A working draft to brainstorm on; open questions are at the bottom.

## The angle

**One designer + AI, shipping agency-quality brand motion — and showing the whole process.**

Most AI-design content shows only the output. The thing nobody else can copy is the *process*: storyboard → comments → build → direct, with your taste steering each round. Every version is a small, complete case study: a premise ("what if eBay Live felt like a live auction show?"), the stills, the comments that changed it, and the result playing live.

Series name: **Motion, by company.** Season 1 = eBay and Google, v1–v3 (six drops).

## Where things live

| Channel | Role | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| **motion.noletorious.com** | The showcase | Every version, playing live. Always the link destination for "see it". |
| **noletorious.com blog** (separate repo) | Home base (canonical) | One post per version. A 16:9 header graphic at the top, a 1920×1080 thumbnail, and links to motion.noletorious.com. |
| **Substack** | Email list, owned audience | A short letter per drop: the story + recap video + link to the case study. Cross-post with the blog as canonical. |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional reach | Native video post (recap) + 3–5 line hook. Link in the first comment, not the post (external links get suppressed). |
| **Reels / TikTok / Shorts** | Discovery | The 9:16 recap, captioned. "Link in bio" → showcase. Recaps live only on social; they don't appear on the website. |

## One version = one drop

Each version ships as a bundle. Most of it is already produced by the build:

1. **9:16 recap** (15s): already in the pipeline. Social only.
1b. **Blog header + thumbnail**: a 16:9 motion graphic for the top of the post, plus a 1920×1080 still of it for the thumbnail and social card.
2. **Case study** (blog, ~800–1,200 words):
   - the premise and why that company or feature
   - storyboard stills, and the 2–3 comments that changed the direction
   - the final pieces, embedded live from the site
   - what the AI did vs what you decided
   - time spent (a surprisingly compelling number)
3. **Process clip** (20–30s screen recording): a comment pinned on /review, then the fix. The "AI + taste" moment people share.
4. **Substack letter**: the story in 300 words + recap + link.
5. **LinkedIn post**: recap video + hook + first-comment link.

## Launch sequence

1. **Before launch:** host the site; set up share images, the newsletter signup and analytics (see "What the site needs").
2. **Launch post:** "Why I'm redesigning brands in motion". The thesis, the workflow, the v1s. Post it on the blog, Substack and LinkedIn the same week.
3. **Drop 1: eBay v1.** Then Google v1. Then the v2s, the first versions that show real range.
4. **Mid-season essay:** "How I direct AI like a motion team" (the three levels, calibrating the skill to your taste). Evergreen, very shareable.

## What the site needs to support this

These are backlog items:
- **Share previews:** an Open Graph image and title per version, so links look great on LinkedIn and Substack.
- **Embed URL:** e.g. `/embed/<company>/<v>/<piece>`, so case studies can play pieces live inside the blog.
- **Case-study page per version** on the site: the storyboard stills, round by round, plus the final pieces. The process becomes part of the portfolio.
- **Newsletter signup** (Substack embed) in the footer and on version pages.
- **Light analytics** (e.g. Plausible) to see which drops bring people in.
- **UTM links** per channel, so you know whether LinkedIn or Reels actually converts.

## Guardrails

- **Spec work, stated plainly** in every post: "unofficial exploration, not affiliated". Tagging the brand is fine; implying they commissioned it is not.
- **Verify every number** before a version goes public. The unverified ones are marked in brand.json.
- **Real logos only** where usage is fair (commentary, portfolio). Keep stand-ins otherwise.

## Measure

Followers and email subscribers per drop, inbound DMs and leads, and which channel sends people to the showcase. Review after 3 drops and double down on what works.
